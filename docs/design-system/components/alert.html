Alert and banner

An alert keeps a message on the page until the situation changes: four tones, an optional dismiss button and actions, and a full-width banner form for page-level and connection status.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableSince v1.0

Overview

Use an alert for a message that belongs to a region of the page and that the person may need to act on or re-read: "Two members have no allowance set", "Only managers can change team settings". Use the banner form (.alert.banner) when the message concerns the whole page or the whole app: connection state ("You’re offline"), a failed save of a settings page, or a saved-confirmation that should stay visible.

It is not a toast (toast is transient and for results), not a field message (inline message) and not a blocking decision (dialog). Do not stack more than one banner; replace the older message instead.

Anatomy

  1. Tone iconInfo, success, warning or danger glyph in --alert-icon, aria-hidden.
  2. TitleA short sentence stating the situation (.alert__title, label weight).
  3. TextOptional explanation and consequence, regular weight.
  4. Actions (optional)Links or buttons in .alert__actions, inheriting the alert colour and underlined as links.
  5. Dismiss (optional)A .close button; omitted when the alert resolves itself.
  6. Tone bar4px left border in the tone's border colour; with the icon and title it is redundant, never the only cue.

Variants

Tone is set by four component tokens (--alert-bg, --alert-fg, --alert-border, --alert-icon) that each tone class overrides. Info is the default.

Sizes

There is one size. An alert fills its container (card, form column or page); the banner fills the viewport. Body text wraps and the dismiss button hangs at the top right, so long messages never push it off screen. Keep the title to one line and the whole message to two sentences; anything longer belongs on its own page.

States

An alert is either visible or gone; it has no hover or loading state of its own. The states that matter are whether it can be dismissed, whether it resolves itself, and how it is announced.

  • Persistent alerts have no dismiss button. They leave when their cause does: the offline banner when the connection returns (painting stays disabled meanwhile), the read-only notice for non-managers when the role changes.
  • Dismissed alerts are removed from the DOM and not shown again for the same cause in the session. Move focus to the next logical control if the dismiss button had it.
  • Success alerts are dismissible and replaced by the next message; they do not time out. Use a toast if you want a transient confirmation.
  • Alerts are static: no entrance animation, so nothing changes under prefers-reduced-motion.

Responsive behaviour

  • The alert is a flex row with --space-3 gap and --space-4 padding at every width; the body wraps, the icon and dismiss button keep their size.
  • The .banner body is a wrapping row (--space-4 gap): title and text sit side by side on wide screens and stack on a phone, centred.
  • Banners sit above the top bar in the page flow, so they push the page down rather than overlapping; do not make them sticky on phones.
  • Actions wrap under the text; each stays at least 24px tall (--target-min) with a gap of --space-3.
  • At 360px a banner of title plus one sentence takes about four lines; shorten the copy before shrinking the type.

Theming

Every tone reads four semantic tokens in the pattern --color-{tone}-bg|fg|border|icon and exposes them through the component tokens --alert-bg, --alert-fg, --alert-border and --alert-icon. Dark mode swaps the tint to the 950 step with a 200–300 foreground, so contrast holds in both themes without a component override. To add a tone, define the four semantic tokens and a modifier that sets the four component tokens.

.alert--brand {
  --alert-bg: var(--color-accent-subtle);
  --alert-fg: var(--color-fg-accent);
  --alert-border: var(--color-accent);
  --alert-icon: var(--color-fg-accent);
}

Accessibility

Role and pattern

ARIA status and alert roles (WAI-ARIA live-region guidance; the APG alert pattern). A static alert present at load needs no role: it is read in order. An alert that appears in response to an action gets role="alert" (assertive) when it reports a failure, or sits inside a role="status" container for neutral updates. Do not add role="alert" to alerts that exist at page load: they would be announced twice.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabMoves through the actions and the dismiss button in DOM order. The alert itself is not focusable.
Enter, SpaceActivates the focused action or the dismiss button.
EscNo effect. An alert is not modal; do not bind Esc to dismiss it.

Focus

Actions and the dismiss button use the shared ring. Actions inherit the alert colour, so the ring keeps its own colour (--color-focus-ring) against the tinted surface. Do not move focus to an alert when it appears; announcing it through the live region is enough. The one exception is a form-level error summary, which receives focus on submit (see inline message).

Labelling

The title is the first thing a screen reader speaks. The tone must be present in words: write "We couldn’t save your settings", not "Error". The icon is decorative and aria-hidden. The dismiss button is named "Dismiss" (add the subject when several alerts are visible: "Dismiss: Team settings saved").

Motion

Alerts do not animate; they appear and disappear. The connection banner's spinner or refresh icon is the only moving element, and a spinner keeps turning under prefers-reduced-motion because it is a status indicator (see spinner). Do not auto-dismiss alerts: a message the person has not read in time would be lost (WCAG 2.2.1). The one exception is the “You’re back online” banner, which leaves after 5 s because it only confirms that the offline banner is over (it also keeps its Dismiss button).

Touch

The dismiss button is --target-min square with a negative margin that keeps the alert compact; actions are text buttons with 8px or more between them. Neither requires a gesture, and the alert never blocks the sheet underneath.

Content

  • Title: state the situation in one short sentence: "You’re offline", "Two members have no allowance set", "Only managers can change team settings".
  • Text: the consequence, then the way forward: "You can look at the calendar, but changes can’t be saved until you’re back."
  • Errors: say what failed and what is safe: "We couldn’t save your settings. Nothing was changed." Never "Error", never a status code alone.
  • Actions: verbs, at most two. Recovery first ("Try again"), explanation second ("What this means").
  • Use curly apostrophes and the product's words: team, member, day, holiday, manager. Reconnecting states end in an ellipsis ("Reconnecting…").
