Components · Actions

ButtonButtons trigger an action on the current page. Seven variants, three sizes, every state, in both themes.Segmented controlA segmented control picks exactly one option from a short, always-visible set. In Greenlit it is the paint picker on the calendar; the same file also holds the joined button group for independent toggles.LinkA link goes somewhere: another page, another section, a mailto. It is an anchor with a real href, underlined so it never relies on colour alone.MenuA menu is a short, anchored list of actions or destinations that opens from a button. In Greenlit there is one: the account menu behind the avatar in the top bar.

Components · Inputs

Text fieldA text field takes a single line of text, an e-mail address, a password, a number or a search term. It always lives in a form field with a visible label.TextareaA textarea takes free text that needs more than one line: a reason for declining a request, a note on a time-off request.SelectA select chooses one value from a closed list of known options. It is the native browser control with Greenlit styling.CheckboxA checkbox is an independent yes/no choice, or one of several choices where any number can be on.Radio groupA radio group picks exactly one option from a short list that stays visible, and submits it with the form.SwitchA switch turns one setting on or off and applies the change immediately. Greenlit uses it for the manager's notification preferences.Form fieldA form field wraps one control with its label, optional marker, help text, error message and counter, and wires them together for assistive technology.Form layoutForm layout arranges fields: a responsive grid, fieldsets with legends, an error summary at the top and an actions row at the bottom.CounterA counter adjusts one small whole number with minus and plus buttons and shows the value between them. Greenlit uses it for how many people must be in on a working day.

Components · Navigation

Top barThe top bar is the app's single global header: logo, the four destinations, the team name and the account menu.App navigationThe app navigation is the four-link row in the top bar: Calendar, Year, Requests and Settings.TabsTabs switch between views of the same list on one screen, such as Pending, Approved and Declined requests.Breadcrumb and back linkBack link and breadcrumb: how a person climbs one level (or several) from a detail screen.PaginationPagination moves through a list that is too long for one screen, such as a long request history.Skip linkThe skip link is the first focusable element on every page; it appears on focus and jumps over the top bar to the main content.Settings navigationSettings navigation is the in-page list of anchors that jumps between the sections of the Settings screen.Period navigationPeriod navigation is the pair of arrows beside the month or year title that steps the sheet one period at a time.

Components · Containers and overlays

CardA card is a bordered, softly raised surface that groups related content, such as the sign-in form or the team sheet.DialogThe dialog is the modal for confirmations and short forms: sign out, remove a member, add a day off, decline a request.DrawerThe drawer is a side panel that opens a record in context; in Greenlit it shows one time-off request with its decision actions.TooltipA tooltip is a short, non-interactive label that names or briefly explains an icon-only control on hover and focus.DividerA divider is a hairline rule that separates groups without adding a container.Layout primitivesLayout primitives are the small classes that compose pages: shell, container, stack, cluster, grid, page title row, toolbar row and section.

Components · Data display

TableTables compare the same facts across people: allowance, days used, days left. Sticky header, numeric columns, optional sort and row actions, loading and empty versions.ListA vertical run of rows that each describe one thing: a member, a request, a stretch of time off. Plain, interactive and run-list forms.Description listTerms and their values: a settings summary as two columns, a member's allowance as a row of facts.AvatarA round badge with a member's initials (or photo) that makes rows and menus recognisable at a glance. Four sizes, a stacked group, and the compact version in the team grid.Badge and statusSmall labels that say where something stands: tone badges, the pending-request count pill, request status with its dot, and the inline save status.ChipA compact pill that filters or toggles: pick the working days, narrow the list to who is out, remove a selected item.StatOne number with its caption: the big days-left figure on a person's page, and the small label-value-delta stat for summaries.Member listThe settings list of everyone on the team: name and email, role, and yearly days, editable in the row by the manager.Request rowOne request on the manager's Requests page: who, which days, the note, anything that clashes, and the decision. Stacks on a phone, four columns from 768px.

Components · Feedback

ToastA toast confirms what just happened without moving the user: a short, transient message on the inverse surface that auto-dismisses unless it reports a failure.Alert and bannerAn alert keeps a message on the page until the situation changes: four tones, an optional dismiss button and actions, and a full-width banner form for page-level and connection status.Inline messageInline messages sit next to the thing they describe: field help, field errors, a form-level error summary and a short saved or failed status.Progress barA progress bar shows how far something has got: a determinate or indeterminate track, plus the segmented usage bar that shows a member's allowance.SpinnerA spinner shows that something short is happening and the app has not frozen: a ring in two sizes, the busy button, and the banner spinner.SkeletonSkeletons hold the shape of content while it loads, so the page does not jump when the real thing arrives: text lines, a title, a circle and a rectangle.Empty stateAn empty state explains why a region has nothing to show and offers the next step: an icon, a title, a sentence and up to two actions, in a default, compact and error form.Error pageError pages replace a whole screen when the app cannot show it: not found, forbidden, server error, offline and session expired, each with one clear next step.

Components · Sheet and brand

Logo lockupThe Greenlit mark and wordmark: a month in miniature, with four cells tracing a check, and the lowercase name with a green dot on the i.Sheet cellOne day for one member: a coloured square that is also a button, whose status is spelled out by a glyph and an accessible name.Team gridThe sheet: members down the left, weekdays and dates across the top, one coloured cell per member per day, and a coverage row under the last member.Year gridTwelve months for every member on one screen: each month is a strip of one-day slivers, so a year's leave reads as colour blocks.Month miniA small calendar month, seven columns wide, with each day coloured by status: used on the person page to show one member's whole year as twelve of these.LegendThe key to the sheet's colours: a row of small swatches with their names, placed directly above the view that uses them.Selection barThe floating toolbar that appears when days are selected on the sheet: it names the selection and offers Confirm, Request, Tentative and Clear.Week tabsThe row of week tabs above the week strip on a phone: one tab per Monday-to-Sunday week of the month, named by its days in the month, with a marker on a week that is short of people.Week stripOne week of the team on a phone: the seven dates as buttons, a row of seven cells per member with their days left, the Out count under each working day, and bands on short days.Mark sheetThe panel fixed to the foot of a phone while days are marked: it names the member and the days picked, asks for the last day, then offers Request, Tentative, Confirm and Clear. It is not a dialog.

How to use

  1. Link the stylesheets: tokens/tokens.css first, then assets/components.css. Set data-theme="dark" on <html> to force dark; without it the OS preference applies.
  2. Use the class names documented on each component page (.btn, .field, .dialog, .cell …). The Angular gl-* components in frontend/projects/components preserve these BEM classes and state attributes, because the e2e locators depend on them.
  3. Read tokens by role: var(--color-fg-muted), never a hex value. Component-scoped knobs keep the --gl- prefix in Angular.
  4. Generate platform constants from tokens/tokens.json (DTCG format). Never edit it by hand: edit tokens/tokens.css and regenerate.
  5. After a token change run check_contrast.py; after a page change run check_design_system.py (both are in the extracting-design-systems skill).
