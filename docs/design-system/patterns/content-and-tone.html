Content and tone

The words Greenlit uses, the formulas for errors and empty states, and the rules for labels, numbers and dates.

Overview

Copy is part of the interface, and consistent words are what makes a shared sheet understandable at a glance. This pattern is the working summary; the full voice rules sit in the Content foundation. Apply it to every button, field, toast, empty state and dialog.

When to use which

WritingFollowTone
Button or link labelVerb phrase, sentence caseDirect
Field label and helpNoun; help gives the reasonPlain
Validation messageField error formulaCalm, instructive
FailureError formula; always say what was keptCalm, never alarming
Empty regionEmpty formulaWelcoming, practical
Result of an actionPast tense, with the people and numbersBrief
ConfirmationQuestion title, outcome buttonsNeutral; serious for removal
Request note and decline reasonThe member's or manager's own wordsNever edited by the product
  • When two formulas apply, the error formula wins: say what to do before anything else.
  • If you need a new word, check this list first; add it here before adding it to the UI.

Structure

Greenlit writes like a careful colleague: short, plain, specific. The product has a small vocabulary and uses it everywhere.

Vocabulary

SayMeansNever say
TeamOne sheet, with one manager, a time zone, its members and its holidaysGroup, workspace, org
MemberA row on the sheet; may be a name only until they sign inUser, employee, staff, person (in UI)
DayOne cell for one member on one dateSlot, entry, record
HolidayA red day for the whole team (weekends are red by rule)Closure, bank holiday, blackout
ManagerThe person who confirms requests and owns holidays and membershipAdmin, approver, owner
TentativeBlue: pencilled in, not yet requestedDraft, maybe, pending
RequestedYellow: waiting on the managerSubmitted, pending
ConfirmedGreen: the manager has said yesApproved (except the button), booked
Working dayA day with no rowEmpty, free, available

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: button labels fit on one line; if one wraps, shorten the verb phrase. Toast titles may take two lines; helper text may take three.
  • 768px: the same words. Do not add words to fill space.
  • 1024px and up: line length for help and body copy is capped near 65ch (--measure).
  • Cell and column headers use short date forms (Fri 6 Nov); the long form goes in the accessible name or title.

Accessibility

  • Write at a plain reading level; avoid idioms. Spell out abbreviations on first use except those in the vocabulary.
  • Link and button text is meaningful out of context: "Open settings", not "Click here". Icon-only controls have an aria-label with a verb: "Dismiss", "Clear selection".
  • Cell accessible names follow "Member, Weekday day Month: Status", e.g. "Ana Ruiz, Tue 24 Nov: Requested".
  • State is always in words somewhere: the legend, the paint picker labels, the cell symbols.
  • Set the page language (lang="en"); mark names or phrases in another language with lang.
  • Do not use all caps in source text; use CSS only for overlines. Do not rely on punctuation or emoji to carry meaning.

Content

  • Casing: sentence case everywhere, including buttons, titles and navigation. Proper nouns and Greenlit keep their capitals.
  • Punctuation: no full stop on titles, labels and buttons; full stops on sentences. Use typographic apostrophes and en dashes in ranges. No exclamation marks.
  • Person: address the user as "you" for their own days ("Your connection is unstable"); name others ("Maya will see it").
  • Tense: results in the past ("Request sent"); the current state in the present; consequences in the future ("Ana will see this").
  • Length: button 1 to 3 words; toast title under 60 characters; help one sentence; error two sentences.
  • Never: "Oops", "Whoops", "Please", "Sorry" (except for something we caused), "successfully", "invalid" alone, "n/a".
