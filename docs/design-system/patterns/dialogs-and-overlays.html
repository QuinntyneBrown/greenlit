Dialogs and overlays

Dialog, drawer and menu: when to interrupt, how to build the interruption, and how focus moves in and out.

Overview

Overlays interrupt. Greenlit uses them only for tasks that need a decision or a few fields, and always through Angular CDK Dialog or Overlay: no hand-built modals. This pattern covers dialog, drawer, menu and tooltip, and ties them to Forms and Notifications.

When to use which

NeedUseBlocks the page?
Short form or a decision (request, invite, add holiday)DialogYes, modal
Irreversible or consequential action (remove, decline, sign out)Confirmation dialog, danger primaryYes
Look at or edit one entity while the sheet stays behindDrawerYes, but the sheet remains visible
Pick one of a few actionsMenu from a buttonNo, dismisses on outside click
Explain a control or a cellTooltip; the same text is in the accessible nameNo
Report that something happenedToast, not a dialogNo
Reversible and instantNo confirmation: do it and offer UndoNo
  • Never open a dialog from a dialog. If a step needs more, make it a screen.
  • Do not confirm what you can undo. Painting and clearing days never ask; removing a member always does.

Structure

Dialogs are built with Angular CDK Dialog; the markup is a backdrop and a .dialog wrapper holding a panel with header, body and footer. Specimens below sit in a bounded stage so they do not cover the page.

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: the dialog is a bottom sheet: full width, rounded top corners, up to the viewport height minus a gap, with a sticky footer. Buttons wrap, the primary last.
  • 768px: centred, 32rem wide (24rem small, 48rem large); the entrance scales in.
  • 1024px and up: the drawer is 28rem on the right; the sheet remains visible and scrolls independently.
  • On short viewports the body scrolls, never the page behind.

Accessibility

  • Role and name: role="dialog" with aria-modal="true", named by the title through aria-labelledby and described by the sentence under it (aria-describedby). CDK Dialog supplies the trap.
  • Focus in: first field (autofocus); for a destructive confirmation focus the safe button. Focus trap: Tab and Shift+Tab cycle inside. Focus out: back to the control that opened the dialog, or to a sensible neighbour if that control no longer exists (the removed member's row).
  • Esc closes unless the dialog is busy or has unsaved input that would be lost; then Esc asks nothing and is ignored while busy. The Close button always works.
  • The page behind is inert and aria-hidden. Scroll is locked on the body.
  • Backdrop click closes only dialogs without input.
  • Menus use aria-haspopup="menu", aria-expanded, arrow-key navigation and Esc to return focus to the button.
  • The close icon button is named ("Close", or "Close" + the dialog name when several could be open in tests).

Content

  • Title asks the question or names the task: "Decline Ana's request?", "Request time off". Confirmation titles end with a question mark.
  • Description says who is affected and what is kept.
  • Buttons: the confirm repeats the title's verb ("Decline request", "Yes, remove Ben"); the safe button says what happens if chosen ("Keep pending"), or "Cancel".
  • Do not put "Are you sure?" in a title.
  • The consequence list, if any, is three bullets or fewer.
